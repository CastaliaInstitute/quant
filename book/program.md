Castalia Institute plans to offer a **MagAI (Quant)** program that uses the Quant. system. Quant. supplies a forkable research foundation: experiment records, configurable simulation environments, RL gym interfaces, explicit assumptions, and reviewable evaluation protocols. The program supplies a learning pathway for using and extending that system to ask and examine quantitative questions.

The program is in development. This page describes its intended relationship to QuantLab; it does not announce enrollment, dates, instructors, credentials, or completed course materials.

## A research apprenticeship

Learners should move from understanding a claim to producing an inspectable experiment:

1. **Frame a question.** State a narrow hypothesis, target, horizon, universe, and conditions that would disconfirm it.
2. **Define the evidence.** Record data provenance, timestamps, feature definitions, and limitations before fitting a model.
3. **Build a comparison.** Evaluate simple baselines with the same decision timing and transaction-cost assumptions.
4. **Validate chronologically.** Separate training, validation, and untouched test periods; preserve the holdout from model selection.
5. **Invite challenge.** Review the result through statistical, economic, engineering, and stewardship perspectives.
6. **Publish a research record.** Preserve the code, assumptions, versions, evidence label, objections, and revisions so another learner can reproduce the work.

The book's live cells are small exercises for learning these habits. They use synthetic observations and a bounded Python subset on a Cloudflare Worker. They are not a full Jupyter kernel, RL training runtime, market-data service, or trading terminal. RL and larger simulations belong in a controlled research environment that a learner can fork and run locally or privately.

## Program and platform have different roles

Quant. should remain useful as an open research system beyond a single course. The MagAI (Quant) program can use its conventions, gyms, and exercises, while each fork and experiment remains independently inspectable. Participation in a program is not evidence that a strategy works; completion of a simulation exercise is not investment advice.

## What the first learning project asks

Learners can inspect the catalyst gym, where literature-informed program advancement and positive/negative stock response are distinct draws, then build event panels from trial-registry records, biomedical publications, dated issuer releases, and licensed stock bars. The AI-impact watch asks whether AI-associated drug programs progress differently, trial operations change, or public evidence changes company value. The first historical price cohort is too small and clustered to train a market model. Expand across therapeutic areas and issuers, curate AI evidence at the molecule level, and use event-level outcomes in historical-replay RL gyms only after timestamped data and honest chronological evaluation exist.
