This reproducible exercise applies Quant.'s multi-asset allocation and backtest primitives to a fixed synthetic biotech/pharma market. It is a portfolio-construction demonstration before RL: first establish how simple constrained policies behave, then require a learned policy to beat the strongest validation-selected rule under the same constraints and costs.

## Experimental contract

- **Evidence label:** synthetic portfolio mechanics only. The 1,500-session path is generated from a fixed seed, correlated market/sector factors, and independently drawn issuer gaps. Neither the price path nor the XBI-like benchmark is historical data.
- **Policies:** cash; equal-weight issuers; 20-session inverse-volatility allocation; and a 20-session trend rule. The synthetic XBI-like sector series is the passive benchmark, not an observed market series.
- **Decision timing:** each row's weights use returns through that close and apply to the next session. The first session remains in cash.
- **Constraints:** long-only, cash permitted, maximum 35% per issuer, and maximum 55% per sector. Volatility and momentum inputs are available at the decision close only.
- **Costs:** proportional costs are charged on absolute traded weight at 5, 10, and 20 basis points per unit turnover. No spread, market impact, latency, or capacity model is included.
- **Comparison:** benchmark-relative compounded wealth uses identical dates and the synthetic XBI-like path. The table also shows net CAGR, maximum drawdown, expected shortfall, and turnover at base cost.

The deterministic report at [`_static/synthetic-portfolio-demo.json`](../_static/synthetic-portfolio-demo.json) is generated by [`scripts/build_synthetic_portfolio_demo.py`](../../scripts/build_synthetic_portfolio_demo.py). Run it after installing the project to reproduce the artifact. The builder uses `allocate_portfolio` and `portfolio_cost_sensitivity`, so this exercise also checks the library path used by a future historical study.

## Interpretation

The policies are fixed illustrations, not fitted or selected strategies. Different synthetic assumptions can reverse their ordering; the comparison has no independent historical regimes and does not support a return or alpha claim. Historical use still requires licensed point-in-time prices, delisted-issuer coverage, an untouched chronological evaluation, realistic execution assumptions, and the same predeclared policy family. RL should be added only after its observation and action spaces enforce these same issuer/sector limits and it is compared against these non-RL baselines.
